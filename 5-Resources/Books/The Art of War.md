# The Art of War — Sun Tzu

## Summary
- Winning is about preparation and positioning before any conflict starts, not about the fight itself.
- The best victory avoids fighting altogether.
- Know both yourself and your opponent; knowing only one leaves you exposed.
- Deception and adaptability beat rigid plans — conditions change, so strategy must too.
- Waste is costly: long campaigns drain resources even when you win.

## Core argument
- Strategy is decided by assessment: compare strengths, weaknesses, terrain, and leadership before acting.
- Avoid direct, costly confrontation; win by making the opponent's position untenable.
- Use surprise and flexibility — "water shapes its course according to the nature of the ground".
- Speed matters: prolonged efforts exhaust even the winner.

## Key concepts
- **Winning without fighting:** the highest skill is achieving the goal without a costly battle.
- **Know yourself, know the enemy:** honest self-assessment plus understanding the other side.
- **Adaptability:** plans follow conditions, not the other way round.

## My highlights

> "If you know the enemy and know yourself, you need not fear the result of a hundred battles." — Ch. 3

## My takeaways
- Most of the work in any competitive situation is the analysis beforehand — negotiations, interviews, business cases.
- Choosing which battles not to fight is a strategy in itself.

## So what for me
- Consulting-style strategy frameworks (competitor analysis, SWOT) are a modern version of "know yourself, know the enemy".
- A useful lens for negotiation: look for the option where nobody has to lose.
